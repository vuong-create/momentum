# Momentum

**Personal Operating System**

Started: July 30, 2026

---

# Mission

Momentum is a personal operating system designed to reduce mental load and help create consistent progress in the areas of life that matter most.

Momentum exists to help me:

* Know what I need to do today
* See what the rest of my week looks like
* Capture thoughts before they disappear
* Organize responsibilities without excessive maintenance
* Stay connected to long-term goals
* See progress across important areas of life
* Preserve memories worth looking back on

The goal is not maximum productivity.

The goal is:

> **Keep moving forward.**

---

# What Momentum Is

Momentum is a personal home base for everyday life.

It combines:

* Weekly planning
* Tasks
* Personal growth tracking
* Finance
* Chinese learning
* Athletics
* Cooking
* Journaling and memories
* Global progression

These systems should feel connected rather than like separate applications.

Momentum should feel like somewhere I naturally want to return to.

---

# What Momentum Is Not

Momentum is not:

* A productivity system that creates more work
* A habit tracker built around guilt
* A game where points matter more than growth
* A system that requires constant logging
* A complicated life-management database
* A replacement for every specialized application

Momentum should only track information when doing so creates meaningful value.

> **The system exists to serve the user.**

---

# Core Philosophy

## Reduce Friction

The best system is the one that actually gets used.

Common actions should require as little effort as possible.

Momentum should make life easier rather than becoming another responsibility.

Before adding a feature, ask:

> **Does the value of this feature justify the effort required to use it?**

If not, it probably does not belong.

---

# Plan → Do → Build Momentum

The primary Momentum loop is:

> **Plan → Do → Complete → See Progress → Repeat**

Planning creates intention.

Doing creates progress.

Momentum makes that progress visible.

---

# The Weekly Planner Is the Engine

The Weekly Planner is one of Momentum's most important systems.

It represents:

> **What do I want this week to look like?**

The planner can contain:

* Workouts
* Chinese activities
* Meals
* Finance tasks
* Reading
* Personal activities
* Errands
* Chores
* Appointments
* General tasks

Everything does not need to belong to a pillar.

The Weekly Planner connects everyday life with Momentum's longer-term progression.

---

# Planning Without Rigidity

Planning should provide direction without making life inflexible.

Tasks can be:

* Moved
* Rescheduled
* Dismissed
* Completed spontaneously
* Added during the week

Missing something does not create punishment.

Momentum should make it easy to adjust and continue.

---

# One Item, Multiple Views

Momentum should avoid duplicate information.

A single underlying activity can appear wherever it is useful.

Example:

**Thursday — Pull Workout**

can appear in:

* Home
* Today
* Weekly Planner
* Athletics
* XP history

These are not separate copies.

Completing the workout once updates every relevant view.

Core principle:

> **Enter once. Update everywhere.**

---

# Home Is the Starting Point

Momentum Home should answer three questions within approximately five seconds:

> **What do I need to do today?**

> **What does the rest of my week look like?**

> **How are the important areas of my life going?**

The Home page is the natural starting point for Momentum.

---

# Home Hierarchy

The primary Home structure is:

## Today

What matters today.

↓

## This Week

The global Weekly Planner.

↓

## Your Momentum

Lightweight summaries of the five life pillars.

↓

## What's on Your Mind?

Frictionless personal thought capture.

Complex analytics belong inside the pillars rather than on Home.

---

# What's on Your Mind?

Momentum includes a frictionless writing space:

## What's on your mind?

This is not an AI chat.

Momentum does not need to respond.

The purpose is simply:

> **Write something → Save it → Move on.**

Entries automatically become part of Happiness / Journal.

No title, category, mood, or tags are required.

---

# Capture Without Friction

Thoughts should be easy to preserve.

Examples:

* Something that happened today
* Something worth remembering
* Something bothering me
* Something I'm thinking about
* An idea
* A reflection
* A memory

Momentum should never require organizing a thought before saving it.

---

# Five Life Pillars

Momentum currently focuses on five major areas:

1. Finance
2. Chinese
3. Athletics
4. Cooking
5. Happiness / Journal

Each pillar has a different purpose.

They should **not** be forced into identical structures.

---

# Finance

Finance answers:

> **How am I doing financially?**

It is intentionally Momentum's most data-heavy pillar.

Primary responsibilities include:

* Transactions
* Budgeting
* Accounts
* Spending
* Savings
* Investments
* Net worth
* Financial progress

Finance can require more detailed entry because detailed financial information provides meaningful value.

---

# Chinese

Chinese answers:

> **Am I consistently keeping Chinese in my life?**

It should remain lightweight.

Primary responsibilities include:

* Quick activity logging
* Chinese exposure
* Personal word/phrase database
* Anki access
* Activity heatmap
* Consistency tracking

Momentum organizes Chinese learning.

It does not need to replace Anki or other dedicated language-learning tools.

---

# Athletics

Athletics answers:

> **Am I training consistently, and am I getting stronger?**

Primary responsibilities include:

* Fast workout logging
* Workout templates
* Exercise history
* PR detection
* Volleyball activity
* Weekly training consistency
* Progress visualization

Rest is normal.

Athletics should not encourage unnecessary daily training simply to maintain a streak.

---

# Cooking

Cooking answers:

> **What am I eating this week, and what do I need to buy?**

Primary responsibilities include:

* Weekly meal planning
* Personal cookbook
* Recipes
* Recipe photos
* Grocery list
* Meal suggestions
* Recently cooked meals

Core flow:

> **Meals → Weekly Plan → Grocery List → Cook**

Cooking should make deciding what to eat easier, not create another database that needs constant maintenance.

---

# Happiness / Journal

Happiness answers:

> **What do I want to capture and remember?**

It is intentionally the lightest Momentum pillar.

Primary responsibilities include:

* Journal entries
* Photos
* Reflection prompts
* Quotes
* Calendar
* On This Day
* Random Memory

The Journal should feel like a personal notebook rather than a productivity application.

Core philosophy:

> **Capture → Reflect → Remember**

---

# Reading

Reading is a first-class Momentum activity but does not require its own pillar.

Reading can:

* Appear in the Weekly Planner
* Appear in Today
* Be recurring
* Earn appropriate XP
* Contribute to long-term Momentum progression

Detailed book tracking is not required for Version 1.

---

# Progress Without Pressure

Momentum should make progress visible without turning life into a scoreboard.

The system should reward:

* Showing up
* Following through
* Meaningful action
* Consistency over time

It should never punish:

* Rest
* Missed days
* Changed plans
* Imperfect weeks
* Returning after time away

There is always a path forward.

---

# XP Philosophy

Momentum uses one global progression system.

All five pillars plus general meaningful activities can contribute toward:

> **One Momentum Level**

XP represents accumulated real-life action.

XP should reward:

* Meaningful activity
* Completed plans
* Consistency
* Long-term follow-through

XP should not reward unnecessary interaction with Momentum.

Core rule:

> **Using Momentum is not the achievement. Doing the thing is.**

---

# Weekly Planning + XP

Planned meaningful activities receive slightly more XP than spontaneous activities.

This rewards:

> **Intention + Follow-through**

without making planning mandatory.

Weekly Planner completion can also generate bonuses.

Momentum therefore operates across three timescales:

### Daily

Do meaningful things.

### Weekly

Follow through on the Weekly Planner.

### Long-Term

Build levels, milestones, badges, titles, and a history of progress.

---

# No Global Daily Streak

Momentum does not require doing something every day.

There is no global:

> **Do something today or lose your streak.**

Different parts of life operate on different schedules.

Rest days matter.

Busy days happen.

Some areas of life do not require daily attention.

Where useful, individual pillars can visualize consistency without controlling global progression.

---

# No Punishment

Momentum does not use:

* Negative XP
* Level loss
* XP decay
* Global streak loss
* Missed-task penalties
* Seasonal progression resets

Missing something simply means:

> **Continue from here.**

---

# Progression Should Mean Something

Momentum levels should become a long-term record of real activity.

Early levels arrive relatively quickly.

Later levels become increasingly meaningful.

Major milestone levels can include:

* Permanent badge
* New title
* Progress snapshot
* Lifetime statistics

Eventually, seeing:

> **Momentum Level 50**

should represent hundreds or thousands of real actions taken over a meaningful period of life.

---

# Personal Progress, Not Competition

Momentum does not need:

* Leaderboards
* Social rankings
* Friend comparisons
* Percentiles

The meaningful comparison is:

> **Me now vs. me before.**

---

# Local-First

Momentum is fundamentally personal.

Core functionality should work locally without requiring:

* An account
* A subscription
* A server
* An internet connection

Personal information such as:

* Finances
* Journal entries
* Photos
* Workouts
* Recipes
* Chinese database
* Progress history

should remain under the user's control.

Backup and export should eventually protect that data.

---

# Different Pillars, One World

Each pillar can have its own functional personality.

**Finance**

Financial command center

**Chinese**

Lightweight learning workspace

**Athletics**

Fast workout logger

**Cooking**

Personal cookbook

**Happiness / Journal**

Personal notebook

They do not need identical interfaces.

They should still feel like different rooms inside the same Momentum world.

---

# Visual Identity

Momentum's overall atmosphere is:

> **A cozy Taiwanese/Japanese-inspired café at night.**

Not cyberpunk.

Not flashy.

Not a generic productivity dashboard.

The application shell reinforces this identity through a narrow icon-only
Momentum Rail. Navigation should feel like a quiet physical instrument: custom
precision-line icons, a faceted geodesic Momentum mark, subtle magnetic
response, restrained pillar light, and ambient presence that changes with the
time of day. Icons retain muted individual color at rest and become brighter on
hover or selection. The rail should never become a wide template-style sidebar
filled with permanent labels.

Visual inspiration includes:

* Warm café lighting
* Rainy city outside a window
* Wooden furniture
* Coffee and tea
* Books
* Notebooks
* Calm Asian city environments
* Anime/cartoon-inspired artwork
* Warm environmental details

The feeling should be:

> **I'm entering my own space to think, plan, and grow.**

---

# Create a World, Not Just a Tool

Momentum should feel like entering a personal space.

The goal is not artificial gamification.

The goal is to make planning, reflection, and self-improvement feel inviting.

Each pillar can interpret the Momentum world differently while maintaining a shared visual identity.

---

# Aesthetics Serve Function

Momentum should be beautiful.

But aesthetics should never make an action harder.

Animations should be:

* Fast
* Subtle
* Satisfying

Sounds should be:

* Optional
* Soft
* Purposeful

Decorative elements should frame information rather than obscure it.

When aesthetics and usability conflict:

> **Usability wins.**

---

# One Source of Truth

Momentum's systems should share underlying data whenever appropriate.

Examples:

A workout completed in Athletics can update:

* Weekly Planner
* Today
* Athletics history
* Weekly consistency
* XP

A planned meal completed in Cooking can update:

* Weekly Planner
* Today
* Recently Cooked
* Cooking history
* XP

One action should produce all relevant consequences automatically.

---

# Automatic Progress

Whenever Momentum already knows something happened, it should calculate the resulting progress automatically.

The user should not need separate actions such as:

> Log workout

then:

> Mark workout complete

then:

> Add XP

then:

> Update weekly progress

One real-world action should require one meaningful interaction.

Momentum handles the rest.

---

# Version 1 Philosophy

Version 1 should prioritize the systems that create the most everyday value.

The goal is not to implement every future idea immediately.

Version 1 should establish:

* Momentum Home
* Weekly Planner
* Shared activity/task system
* Global XP
* Five core pillars
* Reliable local persistence
* Clean navigation
* Backup/export foundation

Advanced features can be added once the core experience is enjoyable and reliable.

---

# Feature Filter

Before adding anything to Momentum, ask:

### 1.

> **Does this make something easier, clearer, or more meaningful?**

### 2.

> **Will I actually use it?**

### 3.

> **Does the value justify the maintenance?**

### 4.

> **Can Momentum calculate this automatically instead?**

If the answer does not justify the feature, leave it out.

---

# Success Criteria

Momentum succeeds if:

* I naturally open it regularly
* I know what I need to do today
* I understand what my week looks like
* It reduces mental clutter
* Planning feels easy
* Important areas of life stay visible
* Progress becomes visible without excessive logging
* Missing a day or week never makes returning difficult
* My data feels like one connected system
* Looking back shows meaningful growth
* The experience feels enjoyable rather than demanding

---

# North Star

Momentum should become:

> **The place I go to see my week, manage the important parts of my life, capture what matters, and keep moving forward.**

It should not make life feel optimized.

It should make life feel **organized, intentional, and remembered.**

---

# Design Rule

Every feature should ultimately answer:

> **Does this help Future Me?**

If not, it probably should not exist.
